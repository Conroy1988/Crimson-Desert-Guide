This route is designed to get a new player through the opening hours with a stable save, readable controls, useful storage, one dependable combat setup and a clear understanding of what can safely wait.

It deliberately avoids quest names, boss identities and story outcomes.

## The short route

Use this order:

1. **Patch and protect the save.** Install the current update, make a manual save and confirm cloud synchronisation before changing device or platform.
2. **Fix readability and camera behaviour.** Set subtitle/font size, camera distance and accessibility options before combat becomes busy.
3. **Learn defence before damage.** Practise movement, dodge, block, lock-on and healing in short encounters.
4. **Complete the opening sequence until normal travel and settlement services are available.** Do not abandon the introduction to explore the whole map immediately.
5. **Run a service circuit.** Locate private storage, a smithy, vendors and the local travel/map unlocks.
6. **Choose one main weapon family.** Use other weapons for testing, but concentrate early refinement and skill learning on equipment you actually use.
7. **Expand carrying capacity and storage gradually.** Requests and camp progress are more valuable than repeatedly selling unknown materials.
8. **Explore around objectives, not instead of them.** Clear the nearby area, then continue the main route before the game has taught its next system.
9. **Treat the first difficult encounter as a mechanics check.** Adjust defence, healing and gear before assuming the answer is more damage.
10. **Delay perfection.** Special mounts, housing, completion cleanup and ideal builds can wait until the core loop feels stable.

## Before starting a new game

### Update first

Patch **1.14.00** is the current verified baseline and is available across Steam, Epic Games Store, PlayStation, Xbox and Mac versions. Starting on the current patch avoids following tutorials or menus that changed after launch.

### Create a save-safety routine

Crimson Desert supports automatic and manual saves. Pearl Abyss recommends saving frequently and maintaining multiple save files. Before a long session:

- keep at least two manual slots;
- do not overwrite the same slot every time;
- allow Steam, PlayStation or Xbox cloud synchronisation to finish before shutting down;
- use the cross-save slot only after linking the intended platform accounts;
- back up local PC saves before major troubleshooting or moving between computers.

See [Save, Storage & Inventory Safety](/start-here/save-storage/) for exact paths and cross-save handling.

## First 15 minutes: make the game readable

Open Settings before judging the combat or interface.

Prioritise:

- **Minimum Font Size** and subtitle size;
- camera visual range, offsets, auto-follow and lookahead;
- controller or keyboard remapping;
- colourblind and photosensitive options where needed;
- Night Tone Mode if dark areas are difficult to read;
- a stable frame-rate target rather than the highest visual preset.

The game has added and revised several accessibility and control options since launch, so older videos may show fewer settings. Use [Essential Settings](/start-here/essential-settings/) as the current reference.

## Opening combat: build control, not a combo list

Your first objective is not maximum damage. It is being able to stop attacking and defend on demand.

Practise this loop:

1. approach one enemy;
2. watch the first attack rather than interrupting it immediately;
3. dodge or block;
4. answer with a short attack burst;
5. stop before stamina or positioning becomes unsafe;
6. reset the camera and repeat.

Avoid long button-mash strings. Crimson Desert has many weapon-specific attacks, grapples and skills, but the universal early skill is controlling when an animation begins and ends.

Before moving on, confirm that you can:

- move and turn without fighting the camera;
- lock on and disengage intentionally;
- dodge without exhausting the resource needed for the next defence;
- block or create distance when surrounded;
- heal without waiting until the last possible moment.

The detailed mechanics belong in [Combat Fundamentals](/systems/combat/). The beginner route only needs a reliable defensive loop.

## When the world opens: perform a settlement circuit

At the first point where normal exploration and services are available, do not leave immediately. Find the functions that will support every later session.

### 1. Reveal local information

In cities, use the bell tower where available to reveal the regional map and nearby points of interest. On the wider map, investigate Mysterious Energy areas because they can contain Abyss Nexus fast-travel points or related discoveries.

### 2. Find private storage

Private Storage was added at the initial temporary lodgings in Hernand and at Howling Hill Camp. Camp expansion can increase capacity from **240 slots to as many as 1,000**.

Deposit materials and equipment you are not currently testing instead of selling them simply to clear space.

### 3. Find a smithy

Smithies are the centre of early equipment progression:

- refinement improves equipment;
- extraction can later reduce refinement levels and return materials;
- special materials are recoverable at a higher rate than common ore and bloodstone;
- extraction does not destroy the equipment itself.

Do not refine every new item. Select one weapon and a small set of armour you expect to use for several encounters.

### 4. Check vendors before selling

Inspect vendor stock, service tabs and any inventory-expansion opportunities before using the sell-all mindset. Recipes, materials, knowledge items and refinement components can look disposable before their systems are introduced.

A safe rule is:

> Sell obvious low-value duplicates and replaceable goods. Store unfamiliar progression materials until their purpose is known.

## Your first equipment policy

### Choose one working loadout

Test available weapons, then select a main weapon family that feels controllable. Your first loadout should give you:

- one dependable close-range option;
- a way to answer distant or elevated threats when available;
- armour with useful survivability rather than a visually perfect set;
- healing and recovery items on accessible controls.

### Refine with a stopping rule

Early refinement uses common resources such as copper and iron ore, while later levels require more valuable materials. Extraction introduced in Patch 1.06.00 makes a poor investment recoverable, but common materials return at roughly **70%**, not 100%.

Stop upgrading when:

- the item is being replaced frequently;
- the next level requires a rare material you have not learned to farm;
- the improvement is solving no current problem;
- you are spreading materials across several unused weapons.

Continue when:

- the item is your current main weapon or core armour;
- a difficult encounter is exposing a real damage or resistance gap;
- the required materials are common and replaceable;
- you understand the extraction loss if you change direction later.

See [Gear, Refinement & Extraction](/systems/gear/) for the full system.

## Exploration without losing the main route

Crimson Desert rewards detours, but unrestricted exploration too early can produce three problems: systems without context, an overloaded inventory and encounters you are not equipped to read.

Use the **one-ring rule**:

- travel to the current objective;
- inspect the immediate paths, ruins, camps, resource nodes and services around it;
- complete nearby requests that offer clear practical rewards;
- return to the main route before crossing into a distant region.

This captures useful discoveries without turning the opening into an unstructured map sweep.

## What to prioritise early

### High priority

- manual saves and cloud-sync checks;
- readable subtitles, font and camera;
- defensive combat fundamentals;
- one main weapon and restrained refinement;
- private storage and inventory expansion;
- map information and Abyss Nexus discovery;
- mounts for repeated long-distance travel;
- smithies and settlement services.

### Medium priority

- requests near the current route;
- gathering common ore and materials;
- cooking or consumables that solve an immediate healing problem;
- learning new skills you can already perform reliably;
- camp expansion that increases storage or operational value.

### Safe to postpone

- perfect builds;
- full-region collectible sweeps;
- rare mount completion;
- housing decoration;
- mass crafting;
- farming an item before you know its long-term use;
- refining every weapon family;
- clearing every stronghold before continuing the story.

## First difficult encounter checklist

Before repeatedly retrying a major fight, check:

- Is the current weapon refined enough to be relevant?
- Is armour badly behind the equipment already found?
- Is healing assigned and stocked?
- Can you identify at least one safe attack window?
- Are you exhausting stamina before the dangerous pattern begins?
- Is lock-on helping, or should you control the camera manually?
- Is the fight exposing a missing mechanic rather than a numerical problem?

Do not spend rare materials after one failed attempt. First change behaviour, then consumables, then equipment.

## First-hours completion checklist

You are ready to leave the beginner phase when you have:

- [ ] created multiple manual saves;
- [ ] confirmed cloud or cross-save behaviour for your platform;
- [ ] configured font, subtitles, camera and accessibility;
- [ ] learned a controlled attack-defend-reset loop;
- [ ] selected one main weapon family;
- [ ] found private storage;
- [ ] visited a smithy and understood refinement versus extraction;
- [ ] stopped selling unknown progression materials automatically;
- [ ] expanded inventory or storage at least once when the opportunity appeared;
- [ ] unlocked or identified a repeatable travel method;
- [ ] used a mount for ordinary travel;
- [ ] completed nearby exploration without abandoning the main route;
- [ ] prepared for a difficult encounter instead of brute-forcing it.
