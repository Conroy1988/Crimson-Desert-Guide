## Confirmed shop intelligence

### Hernand Saddlery

Patch 1.06.00 identifies Hernand as the saddlery for **Wolf** and **Bear** special-mount saddles. These saddles are separate equipment; special mounts no longer display a saddle as part of their default appearance.

Use Hernand when:

- a Wolf or Bear has already been registered;
- the mount needs its own equipment rather than character or horse tack;
- comparing special-mount equipment before committing a long overland route.

### Provisioner's Shop

Patch 1.11.00 added the **Flower Basket** to the Hernand Provisioner's Shop. The guide records this as a confirmed stock change, not as an essential purchase.

### Equipment Shop

Patch 1.11.00 added **Ranged Weapons of the World - Bows, Vol. I** to the Hernand Equipment Shop. Check the shop when auditing books, knowledge or ranged-equipment availability.

## Smithy workflow

The smithy should be treated as a decision point, not a routine upgrade station:

1. make a manual save;
2. compare the proposed item against the active character and task;
3. confirm whether identical-equipment refinement is still available at the current level;
4. inspect any Abyss Artifact requirement;
5. choose between refine, extract, store or leave unchanged;
6. verify the result before continuing.

See [Gear, Refinement & Extraction](/systems/gear/) for the complete material decision model.

## Storage and restock policy

Deposit:

- duplicate weapons or armour kept for comparison;
- special refinement materials;
- mount food not needed on the current route;
- books, quest-adjacent items or unusual equipment whose value is unclear;
- resources collected for later settlement or camp work.

Carry:

- one deliberate combat loadout;
- the healing and mount food needed for the next route;
- only the refinement materials being used immediately;
- enough free capacity for rewards and gathered materials.

## Route-planning value

Hernand is most useful as a **reset point** between activities. Before leaving, answer four questions:

1. Where is the nearest activated travel return point?
2. Is the next activity combat, gathering, exploration or mount handling?
3. Is the active character carrying the correct equipment and resistance profile?
4. Is there enough free inventory space to complete the route without destroying or selling uncertain items?

A route is not efficient if it saves two minutes of travel but forces a long return because storage, mount equipment or healing was ignored.
